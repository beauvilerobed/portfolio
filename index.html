Case study · AniPoint client work

Causal Impact of a New Location

Question
A client asked how much new revenue one more car wash would actually add to a county, as opposed to revenue it would just take from existing sites.
Metric
Incremental county revenue per added site, holding population, income, traffic, and competitors fixed. It is a county-level effect, not a forecast for one address.
Method
Double ML and causal forests on a 12-year county panel, cross-checked with a two-way fixed-effects difference-in-differences on staggered openings and a synthetic-data test that had to recover a known effect.
Result
Both methods put the effect at about $330K–$400K per site, with heavily overlapping intervals. Reports give the range, not a single number.
Caveats
With only one control, the Double ML estimate was roughly twice as large. Adding demand controls brought it in line with the DiD, a reminder that confounding is the whole game. TWFE can be biased when openings are staggered, so it is the cross-check and Double ML is the headline.

Research

Dissertation: A Minimal Invariant Set, Frames, and Operators, University of Pittsburgh

Fixed Point Theory

Building on Alspach's fixed-point-free non-expansive map, showed that special closed linear spans of a minimal invariant set contain a copy of L1[0,1].

Frames & Generalized Frames

Used operator theory to characterize frames in terms of Riesz bases, and generalized frames (g-frames) in terms of g-Riesz bases.

Frame Potentials & Optimization

Studied finite normalized tight frames as minimizers of a potential energy function, and their links to sparse approximation and deep learning.

In plain terms: years of proving exactly when a structure exists and when it breaks. That is the same discipline as checking whether a metric or a causal estimate holds up.
