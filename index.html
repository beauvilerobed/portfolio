Case study · AniPoint client work

Causal Impact of a New Location

Question
A client asked how much new revenue one more car wash would actually add to a county, as opposed to revenue it would just take from existing sites.
Metric
Incremental county car wash revenue per added site, net of cannibalization, holding population, income, traffic, and competitors fixed. Revenue is a payroll-based proxy built from Census County Business Patterns. It is a county-level effect, not a forecast for one address.
Method
Double ML on a 234-county County Business Patterns panel (2012–2023, six states), with county-clustered intervals, cross-checked with a Callaway–Sant’Anna difference-in-differences (opening counties compared only with not-yet-opened ones) and a synthetic-data test that had to recover a known effect.
Result
Double ML gave $533K per added site (95% CI $390K–$676K) and the DiD gave $472K ($281K–$669K), within 1.13× of each other. The DiD’s placebo pre-trend check covers zero, so there’s no sign that opening counties were already pulling ahead. Reports give a range, not a single number.
Caveats
With only one control, Double ML said $795K. Adding five demand controls cut it by a third and brought it in line with the DiD, a reminder that confounding is the whole game. An earlier two-way fixed-effects cross-check ran 1.4–3.7× low because staggered openings used already-treated counties as controls, so I replaced it. Local shocks that drive both openings and revenue (a new highway, a big employer) remain the main untested threat.

Research

Dissertation: A Minimal Invariant Set, Frames, and Operators, University of Pittsburgh

Fixed Point Theory

Building on Alspach's fixed-point-free non-expansive map, showed that special closed linear spans of a minimal invariant set contain a copy of L1[0,1].

Frames & Generalized Frames

Used operator theory to characterize frames in terms of Riesz bases, and generalized frames (g-frames) in terms of g-Riesz bases.

Frame Potentials & Optimization

Studied finite normalized tight frames as minimizers of a potential energy function, and their links to sparse approximation and deep learning.

In plain terms: years of proving exactly when a structure exists and when it breaks. That is the same discipline as checking whether a metric or a causal estimate holds up.
